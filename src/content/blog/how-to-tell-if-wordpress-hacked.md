A hacked WordPress website doesn't always look hacked.

Sometimes the signs are obvious, such as a defaced homepage or a redirect to an unfamiliar website. In other cases, malware can remain hidden while your website continues to look completely normal to you.

Recognizing the warning signs early can help you investigate the problem before it causes more damage.

## Common Signs Your WordPress Website Has Been Hacked

### 1. Your Website Redirects Visitors

If visitors are unexpectedly redirected to another website, a suspicious page, or a search result, your WordPress installation may have been compromised.

Some redirects only happen on mobile devices, specific browsers, or when visitors arrive from search engines. This can make the problem difficult to notice.

### 2. Strange Pages or Posts Appear

Finding unfamiliar pages, posts, categories, or links in your WordPress dashboard is a major warning sign.

Attackers often create spam pages to promote unwanted products, services, or websites.

### 3. Japanese or Other SEO Spam Appears

Japanese SEO spam is a common type of WordPress compromise. You may suddenly discover hundreds of pages containing Japanese text, strange product names, or links to unrelated websites.

These pages may not appear in your normal WordPress navigation but can still be indexed by search engines.

### 4. Google Shows a Security Warning

![Laptop screen showing a malicious file security warning](/images/blog/google-security-warning.webp)

You may see warnings such as:

* This site may be hacked
* Deceptive site ahead
* This site may harm your computer
* Security issues detected

These warnings should be taken seriously. They can indicate that Google has detected potentially harmful or compromised content.

### 5. Unknown Administrator Accounts Appear

Check **Users → All Users** in WordPress.

If you find an administrator account that you didn't create, your website may have been compromised.

However, simply deleting the account isn't enough. An attacker may have created another way to access the website.

### 6. Your Website Suddenly Becomes Slow

A hacked website can consume additional server resources because malicious scripts may be running in the background.

Unexpected CPU or memory usage, slow admin pages, or sudden traffic spikes can be signs worth investigating.

### 7. Your Website Sends Suspicious Emails

If your website suddenly sends large numbers of emails or your hosting provider complains about outgoing spam, your WordPress installation or hosting account may have been compromised.

### 8. Files You Don't Recognize Appear

![Close-up of unfamiliar code and script files on a terminal screen](/images/blog/suspicious-files-code.webp)

Unexpected PHP files, modified WordPress files, or unfamiliar files in plugin, theme, or upload directories can indicate an infection.

Don't delete files simply because you don't recognize them. Some legitimate plugins and WordPress components contain files that may look unfamiliar.

### 9. Your Website Is Defaced

A changed homepage, political message, strange image, or attacker's message is one of the most obvious signs of a hack.

A defaced website may also have deeper malware or backdoors that aren't visible on the homepage.

### 10. Your Hosting Provider Suspends the Website

Hosting companies may suspend websites because of malware, spam, excessive resource usage, phishing content, or other security problems.

If your host has suspended your website, contact them to understand the reason before making major changes.

## How to Check If WordPress Has Been Hacked

![Padlock resting on a backlit computer keyboard](/images/blog/wordpress-security-lock.webp)

Start by looking for unexpected changes:

1. Check your WordPress users.
2. Review recently modified files.
3. Look for unfamiliar plugins and themes.
4. Check your posts and pages for spam.
5. Review redirects.
6. Check your hosting account for unusual activity.
7. Review security warnings from Google or your hosting provider.
8. Scan the website for malware.

A visual inspection alone isn't enough. Malware can be hidden inside legitimate-looking files or database content.

## What Not to Do If You Suspect a Hack

Don't immediately:

* Delete random files
* Delete all plugins
* Reinstall WordPress without investigation
* Change large portions of the website
* Restore an unknown backup
* Ignore the problem because the website still looks normal

These actions can sometimes remove evidence or leave the underlying compromise untouched.

## What Should You Do Next?

If you have strong evidence that your WordPress website has been compromised, the next step is to investigate the infection and determine its scope.

A proper investigation should look beyond the visible symptoms and check for malicious files, backdoors, database changes, unauthorized users, and other persistence mechanisms.

## Final Thoughts

The earlier a WordPress compromise is discovered, the easier it can be to contain and investigate.

If you notice unexpected redirects, spam pages, unknown users, suspicious files, security warnings, or other unusual behavior, don't ignore the signs.

Your website may still be recoverable—but the first step is finding out what actually happened.
